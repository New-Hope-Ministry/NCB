Conflicting Differences:

This testimony comes from a Wednesday morning Bible study that I attended in which 2 Thessolonians 2:1-12 was studied. I was given a paper with the text from these verses on it and I took it home so I could compare it to what I had written for the Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible that God has had me working on since 2018. First I read through the text from the paper I was given, then I read through the text in the Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible that I am working on, and what I found in 2 Thessalonians 2:7 concerned me a bit. So I have written these 3 different versions of this verse here, and I have included the exact punctuation and word capitalization for each version. I do not know which Bible version the verse text on this paper is from, but it reads, "for the mystery of lawlessness is already at work; only He who now restrains will do so until He is taken out of the way." The verse text in the Twenty-First Century Version reads, "For the mystery of wickedness is already at work, but the one who now allows can only allow until he is taken away." And the verse text in the King James Version reads, "For the mystery of iniquity doth already work, only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way."

The first conflicting difference that I noticed, was that in the King James Version the word "he" is not capitalized, but the word "he" is capitalized in the verse printed on the Bible study paper. This immediately caused a conflict between these two versions because of the ancient capitilization format of the ancient texts. The Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible does not use the old capitalization format used by the old archaic versions when speaking about God. Instead it tries to use proper English punctuation and capitalization throughout, and where God or Jesus is referenced by a capitalized pronoun such as He, Him, or His, I try to substitute the proper names of "God", "the Lord", "Holy Spirit", "Son of Man", or "Jesus" to prevent confusion. This requires much prayer before God gives the proper words that he wants written, and I am sure that I still get it all wrong sometimes. I have been reading the Bible since I was 6 years old, and I did not realize and no one told me, that the pronouns for God were all capitalized until I was maybe in my 60's, and I find it very confusing. But since the beginning, God has made it clear to me that he wants a Bible written with proper English, punctuation, and word capitalization so that people like me are not confused by God's word in the Bible.

After I read the different versions, I saw a conflicting difference between verse 7 on the paper that I was given and verse 7 where the word "allows" was used in the Twenty-First Century Version and the word "restrains" was used in the Bible study version. So I decided to look into this further and I prayed about it. The Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible is rooted in the King James Bible, because out of all the ancient translations, the King James Version is the one that people gave their lives to translate. So I decided to compare verse 7 to other Bible versions, and I discovered that the American King James Version, the Authorized King James Version, and the King James Version all use the word "let" where the word "restrains" is used on the paper. I have found that verse 7 in other ancient English language versions of the Bible use the words "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", and "holding him back", which I feel are in direct conflict with the King James Version.

So I used the Online Etymology Dictionary to see if the word "let" could mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", or "holding him back" way back in 1611 when the King James Bible was written. And really the answer is no, "let" did not mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", or "holding him back" in the past. But the Online Etymology Dictionary stated that in those days, the word "let" could mean "to allow". I use many different non-copyrighted English language versions of the Bible for my work with the Bible. And when the Twenty-First Century Version is caught between versions in interpretation, I always pray about it and trust the Holy Spirit. All this prayer and waiting for God's answer is why the Twenty-First Century Version is over 8 years old and it is still not complete.

The Final Conclusion:

After spending the day praying over this simple thing, the Holy Spirit made this revelation to me. First I was moved to change part of what I had previously written in verse 7, and I started to change the word "allows" to "restrains" because I thought I had made a mistake. But the Holy Spirit stopped me and said, the word "let" is the correct word and the word "restrains" is not the correct word for this verse and this is why; "let" means "allow" in the old KJV text and the man of sin and the son of perdition do not want you to be restrained from committing sin. Therefore, the KJV is correct and, "the one who now allows can only allow until he is taken away." Because only Satan has the permission to "allow" us to sin until God removes that permission from him. And it also appears that the use of the capitalized word "He" is an attempt by the deceiver to make God the one who "allows" sin, so that God can be blamed for the sin. And when our long suffering, loving, and slow to anger perfect God is finally fed up and he can stand no more suffering, he will remove all permission to "allow" sin from the evil one. Then the son of perdition will have the man of sin trapped in his sin because he was "allowed" to sin. And since God makes all things, both good and bad work together for God's good grace, the trapped man of sin who was "allowed" to sin, will no longer be "allowed" to sin because Satan's permission to "allow" him to sin has been taken away. Then the man of sin will be revealed, and the son of perdition can betray and condemn the man of sin because of his sin, unless the man of sin returns to God and is saved through Jesus Christ.

In conclusion, I must say that it is because of what Matthew 16:1-12 says, that I know that the Holy Spirit speaks to me, and the Holy Spirit tells me, "When in doubt, trust the Holy Spirit because he will not deceive you."
