2 Thessalonians 2

Author:  God's anonymous servant
Date Published: 08-03-2026

Forgive us father, for I know not what I do! I am going to start this testimony with a link to an etymology dictionary which is a tool for the study of the origin of words and the way in which their meanings have changed throughout history. Online Etymology Dictionary - "Let" 


This testimony began from a morning Bible study that I attended in which 2 Thessolonians 2:1-12 were studied. I was given a paper with this text on it and I took it home so I could compare it to what I had written for the Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible that God has had me working on since 2018.

First I read through the text from the paper I was given, then I read through the text in the Bible version that I am working on, and what I found in 2 Thessalonians 2:7 concerned me a bit. The text on the paper reads, "for the mystery of lawlessness is already at work; only He who now restrains will do so until He is taken out of the way." The text in the Twenty-First Century Version reads, "For the mystery of iniquity is already at work, but the one who now allows can only allow until he is removed from the way." The text in the King James Version reads, "For the mystery of iniquity doth already work, only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." The difference I see is between the two verses are the words "restrains" and "allows". I decided to look into this further so I prayed about it and I discovered that the American King James Version, the Authorized King James Version, and the King James Version all use the word "let" where the word restrains is used on the paper. Other versions use the words "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", and "holding him back".

So I used the Etymology Dictionary to see if the word "let" could mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", or "holding him back" way back in 1611 when the King James Bible was written. And really the answer is no, "let" did not mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", and "holding him back" in the past. I use many different non-copyrighted Bible versions in my work on the Bible, and when I run into a discrepency in translations I fall back on the King James Version, because out of all the ancient translations, the King James version is the one people died to translate.

So after spending the day praying over this simple thing, the Holy Spirit made this revelation to me. First I had to change part of what I had written previously, and I started to change the word "allows" to "restrains" but the Holy Spirit stopped me and said, "let" means "allow" in the old text, and this is why, the man of sin and the son of perdition do not want you restrained from committing sin, therefore "the one who allows, will allow until he is removed." Then the son of perdition will have the unbeliever by the neck. Satan does not want man's sinful nature restrained, and he always looks for ways to use our words against us.
