Forgive us father, for we know not what we do! Feeling depressed and unworthy, I had decided to delete all of my God given testimonies from the internet, until this morning 08-04-2026, at breakfast when I overheard someone say, "He is always trying to make everything all about himself because he just wants to be the center of attention!" I thought they were talking about me, then the Holy Spirit whispered in my ear, "They are not talking about you, they are talking about God." So I decided not to delete all of my God given testimonies from the internet, because I always want God to be the center of attention, so here it is. I am going to start this testimony with a link to an etymology dictionary which is an English tool for the study of the origin of words and the way in which their meanings have changed throughout history. Hopefully this dictionary is edited by humans and is not AI generated which can so easily be corrupted by AI Data Poisoning.

I just discovered the "Online Etymology Dictionary" yesterday 08-03-2026 and it is the only one I could find that was not entirely eat up with advertisement.


This testimony comes from a Wednesday morning Bible study that I attended in which 2 Thessolonians 2:1-12 was studied. I was given a paper with the text from these verses on it and I took it home so I could compare it to what I had written for the Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible that God has had me working on since 2018.

First I read through the text from the paper I was given, then I read through the text in the Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible that I am working on, and what I found in 2 Thessalonians 2:7 concerned me a bit. So I have written these different versions of this verse here and I have included the exact punctuation and word capitalization for each version. I do not know which Bible version the verse text on this paper is from, but it reads, "for the mystery of lawlessness is already at work; only He who now restrains will do so until He is taken out of the way." The verse text in the Twenty-First Century Version reads, "For the mystery of wickedness is already at work, but the one who now allows can only allow until he is taken away." And the verse text in the King James Version reads, "For the mystery of iniquity doth already work, only he who now letteth will let, until he be taken out of the way." I could not help but notice that the word "he" is not capitalized in the King James Version of the Bible but it was capitalized in the verse printed on the Bible study paper. The Twenty-First Century Version of the Bible does not use the old capitalization format used in the old archaic versions, instead it tries to use proper English punctuation and capitalization throughout, and where God or Jesus is referenced by a pronoun such as He, Him, or His, I try to substitute the proper names of "God", "the Lord", "Holy Spirit", or "Jesus" to prevent confusion. This requires much prayer before God gives the proper words that he wants written. But I am sure that I still get it all wrong sometimes. But since the beginning, God has made it clear to me that he wants a Bible written with proper English, punctuation, and word capitalization so that people like me are not confused by God's word in the Bible.

After I read the different versions, I saw a difference between verse 7 on the paper that I was given and verse 7 in the Twenty-First Century Version where the word "restrains" was used in the Bible study version and the word "allows" was used in the Twenty-First Century Version that I am working on. So I decided to look into this further and I prayed about it, then I discovered that the American King James Version, the Authorized King James Version, and the King James Version all use the word "let" where the word "restrains" is used on the paper. I have found that verse 7 in other Bible versions use the words "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", and "holding him back", which I feel are in direct conflict with the King James version of the Bible.

So I used the Etymology Dictionary to see if the word "let" could mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", or "holding him back" way back in 1611 when the King James Bible was written. And really the answer is no, "let" did not mean "restraineth", "restrains", "holdeth", "withholding", or "holding him back" in the past. But the Etymology Dictionary stated that in those days, the word "let" could mean "to allow". I use many different non-copyrighted English versions of the Bible for my work with the Bible. And when the Twenty-First Century Version is caught between versions in interpretation, I always fall back on the King James Version when I run into a discrepency in translations. Because out of all the ancient translations, the King James version is the one that people gave their lives to translate.

So after spending the day praying over this simple thing, the Holy Spirit made this revelation to me. First I was moved to change part of what I had previously written, and I started to change the word "allows" to "restrains" but the Holy Spirit stopped me and said, "let" means "allow" in the old text, and this is why; the man of sin and the son of perdition do not want you restrained from committing sin, therefore "the one who now allows can only allow until he is taken away." Because Satan only has the power to allow us to sin that God has given him, and when our slow to anger God is finally fed up, he removes that power from the evil one. Then the son of perdition will have the man of sin who has been trapped and "allowed" to sin by the neck. And since God makes all things good and bad to work together for his good, the trapped man of sin who was allowed to sin, may return to God and be saved by Jesus.

In conclusion, I want to say that I do not want to argue with the well trained scribes who have all studied the ancient Greek, Hebrew, and Aramaic scriptures who say that the King James Version had many errors in word translations at that time. Because I have no doubt that I would lose that worldly argument to the well educated and degreed college or seminary professionals. And because of Matthew 16:1-12, I know that the Holy Spirit speaks to me, and the Holy Spirit tells me, "When in doubt, go with the King James Version." Furthermore, the evil one does not want man's sinful nature "restrained," for he is always looking for ways to use our words against us."
