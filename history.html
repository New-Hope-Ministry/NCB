Versions History

This is a short history of each version of the Bible in "The Ark Bible" project. A humble effort to preserve as many versions of God's word as I can. There are huge differences between each version of the Bible. I have found over 2,800 versions of the Bible in many different languages on the internet. The Bibles versions in "The Ark Bible" project are either in the public domain or they carry a Creative Commons or MIT copyright. The public domain version's copyrights are either expired due to their age, or they were placed in the Public Domain by their authors. The "King James Version" carries a perpetual copyright owned by the royal family of England in the United Kingdom, but most other countries do not recognize perpetual copyrights. The Creative Commons licenses can be viewed at the Creative Commons Website, the MIT licenses can be found at the MIT Website, and an overview and explanation of the Public Domain can be viewed here Public Domain Explained.



Albanian Bible - Shqip Bibla (ALB)

✍️Authors: Gjon Buzuku, Vangjel Meksi, Kostandin Kristoforidhi, Dom Simon Filipaj, Stephen Etches, and other institutional contributors from the Albanian Bible Society - Shoqëria Biblike Shqiptare.
📅Published: 1994

📜The Albanian Bible, known as the Shqip Bibla, has a rich history of translation efforts spanning centuries, with contributions from several key figures and organizations. Early translators includes Gjon Buzuku (1554-1555), a Catholic priest who translated portions of Scripture into Albanian in Meshari (The Missal), the first known book in Albanian. Vangjel Meksi (1821-1827) translated the New Testament into the Tosk dialect, supported by the British and Foreign Bible Society. Kostandin Kristoforidhi (1868-1884), translated the New Testament into both Gheg and Tosk dialects and parts of the Old Testament. His work laid a foundational linguistic and theological framework. Dom Simon Filipaj (1977 NT, 1994 full Bible), a Roman Catholic scholar from Montenegro who worked with the United Bible Societies to produce the first full Bible in Standard Albanian, rather than a dialect. Stephen Etches (1993), led the European Christian Mission's effort to produce the first bound copy of the entire Bible in Standard Albanian. The Albanian Bible Society (Shoqëria Biblike Shqiptare) was founded in 1974, this organization completed the full Bible translation by 1994. It involved a team of linguists, theologians, and editors who worked to ensure accuracy and literary quality.

American King James Version (AKJ)

✍️Author: Michael Peter (Stone) Engelbrite
📅Published: 1999

📜The American King James Version (AKJ) is not a new translation, but it is a revision of the classic 1611 King James Version (KJV) by Michael Peter (Stone) Engelbrite. Released in 1999, the American King James Version (AKJ) goal was to bridge the gap between the beautiful, but sometimes archaic English of the King James Version (KJV), and the speech patterns of modern Americans. Engelbrite believed in staying faithful to the original meaning, so he focused on updating spellings and vocabulary rather than changing sentence structure or grammar. This kept the familiar and beloved style of the KJV while making it more accessible for contemporary readers. Engelbrite released the AKJ into the public domain, which allows anyone to freely copy, distribute, or even modify it, as long as the core message remains unchanged.

American Standard Version (ASV)

✍️Authors: Phillip Schaff and others.
📅Published: 1901

📜The American Standard Version (ASV) of the Bible, began in 1872 as a project to revise the 1611 King James Version. Phillip Schaff, assembled a team of 30 American and British scholars representing various Protestant denominations, and their efforts culminated in the publication of the revised New Testament in 1881 and the Old Testament in 1885, which was published in Great Britain as the Revised Version. However, the American scholars had suggested changes that were not included in the final British version. Consequently, they released their own version, the ASV, the New Testament was published in 1900 and the complete Bible in 1901. The ASV made use of the Westcott and Hort Greek New Testament and the Masoretic Text for the Old Testament, and it was the first major English translation to use "Jehovah" instead of God or Lord, as the Hebrew translation of YHWH. The ASV was popular at first, but it's archaic language limited its widespread adoption. It later served as the foundation for several 20th-century Bible translations, including the Revised Standard Version, the Amplified Bible, and the New American Standard Bible. Its commitment to a word-for-word translation philosophy has shaped modern Bible translations, balancing the need for literal accuracy with readability, and it continues to influence religious study and translation efforts.

Authorized King James Version (AKV)

✍️Authors: Richard Bancroft, and the archbishop of Canterbury, and 47 other scholars and clergy.
📅Published: 1611

📜The Authorized King James Version (AKV) began in early 17th century England, when King James I, authorized a new Bible translation to address religious controversies and solidify his own rule. Dozens of scholars spent years working on the project, drawing from existing English translations and ancient Greek and Hebrew texts. The resulting Authorized King James Version (AKV) was published in 1611. The Authorized King James Version (AKV)'s beautiful and enduring language quickly gained popularity. It became the standard English Bible for centuries, shaping not just religious practice but also literature and law. Its influence on the English language is undeniable, introducing many phrases and expressions still in use today. The terms "Authorized King James Version" (AKV) and "King James Version" (KJV) are often used interchangeably, but there are very subtle differences. King James never officially "authorized" the translation, even though he did commission it. In practice, the King James Version (KJV) typically refers to the 1769 edition, which saw minor revisions from the 1611 original. The Authorized King James Version (AKV) might specifically reference the 1611 edition or editions very close to it. Overall, the differences between the Authorized King James Version (AKV) and the King James Version (KJV) are very minor. The differences are mostly punctuation or capitalization, and both are based on the original 1611 edition.

Berean Standard Bible (BSB)

✍️Authors: Dr. Gary Hill, Dr. Maury Robertson, Dr. Eugene H. Merrill, Dr. Ulrik Sandborg-Petersen, Dr. Baruch Korman, and Dr. Grant Osborne. T, and others.
📅Published: 2016

📜The Berean Standard Bible (BSB) is a modern translation of the Holy Scriptures that emerged from a commitment to clarity and fidelity to the original Hebrew and Greek texts. Published in 2016, the BSB was developed by a team of scholars associated with Bible Hub and the Discover Bible. This translation is unique in its approach, merging the Berean Literal Bible and the Berean Interlinear Bible to create a version that is both accurate to the original languages and accessible to the modern reader. The Berean Standard Bible (BSB)'s name is inspired by the noble-minded Bereans of Acts 17:11, who diligently examined the Scriptures. Emphasizing this spirit of inquiry, the Berean Standard Bible (BSB) offers a transparent text that encourages study of the root meanings of words. The BSB stands out for its balance between "word for word" and "thought for thought" translation philosophies, maintaining original gender designations and consistency with the core meanings of the source texts. As language evolves, so does the Berean Standard Bible (BSB), with its translation process remaining open to refinement to ensure continued relevance and accuracy.

Bishop's Bible (BBB)

✍️Authors: Archbishop Matthew Parker, William Alley, Edmund Grindal, Richard Cox, and James Pilkington.
📅Published: 1568

📜The Bishop's Bible (BBB), first published in 1568, was an English translation mandated by the Church of England to supplant the Geneva Bible, whose Calvinist-influenced annotations were deemed unsuitable by the Anglican clergy. Archbishop Matthew Parker spearheaded the project, which involved various bishops and scholars who each translated a section, and were identified by their initials in early editions. The authors included William Alley the Bishop of Exeter, Edmund Grindal the Bishop of London (later Archbishop of York and Canterbury), Richard Cox the Bishop of Ely, and John Jewel the Bishop of Salisbury. This translation was notably more formal and less accessible than its predecessor. Despite revisions in 1572, the BBB never achieved the popularity of the Geneva Bible. However, it significantly influenced the King James Version (KJV), with the 1602 edition of the Bishop's Bible serving as its base text. The King James Version (KJV) was completed in 1611, and went on to become one of the most enduring and revered translations in the English-speaking world.

Coverdale Bible (CBV)

✍️Authors: Myles Coverdale.
📅Published: 1535

📜The Coverdale Bible (CBV), completed by Myles Coverdale and published in 1535, holds the distinction of being the first complete Bible in Modern English and the first printed edition in the language. Coverdale's translation was groundbreaking, as it included both the Old and New Testaments. The work was not entirely original; it was heavily based on William Tyndale's earlier translations of the New Testament and parts of the Old Testament. For the remaining sections, Coverdale utilized a variety of sources, including Latin Vulgate, Martin Luther's German Bible, and the Swiss-German Zürich Bible. The 1535 edition's place of publication was initially disputed, but it is now attributed to Antwerp by Merten de Keyser. The CBV was later incorporated into the Great Bible of 1540 and remained influential in various religious texts, including several editions of the Book of Common Prayer. Over 20 editions of the CBV or its New Testament were published, with the last appearing in 1553.

Darby English Bible (DBY)

✍️Authors: John Nelson Darby.
📅Published: 1890

📜The Darby English Bible (DBY), an English translation by John Nelson Darby, was first published in 1890. Darby, an Anglo-Irish Bible teacher associated with the Plymouth Brethren, aimed to produce a modern translation for those without access to the original languages of Scripture. His translation began with the New Testament, published in 1867, followed by revisions in 1872 and 1884. Darby's work was not intended for public reading but for private study, and he typically used the King James Version in his oral ministry. The complete DBY, including the Old Testament translated by his students from his French and German versions, was published posthumously. Darby's translation is noted for its literal approach and for using "Jehovah" for the name of God, diverging from the common practice of using "LORD" or "GOD" in capital letters. This translation has influenced several other versions and remains a significant work for biblical scholars and students. The Darby English Bible (DBY) complete text, including Darby's 3rd edition New Testament and his students' Old Testament, was first published in 1890.

Douay-Rheims Bible (DRB)

✍️Authors: William Allen, Richard Bristow, Thomas Worthington, William Reynolds, and others. Later Bishop Richard Challoner revised it.
📅Published: 1582

📜The Douay-Rheims Bible (DRB), a translation from the Latin Vulgate into English, was a monumental work undertaken by English Catholic scholars in exile. The New Testament was first published in Reims, France, in 1582, followed by the Old Testament in two volumes, released in 1609 and 1610 by the University of Douai. This translation was not only a religious text but also a political statement, supporting the Counter-Reformation during a time when Protestantism was gaining ground in England. The translation included extensive commentary and notes, aiming to uphold Catholic tradition. The Douay-Rheims Bible underwent a significant revision by Bishop Richard Challoner between 1749 and 1752, which improved its readability and accuracy by comparing it with Hebrew and Greek texts. This revised version, known as the Douay-Rheims-Challoner Bible, became the standard for English-speaking Catholics well into the 20th century. It holds the distinction of predating the King James Version and influencing it, despite the latter's attempt to distance itself from Catholic translations.

Geneva Bible (GNV)

✍️Authors: William Whittingham, Myles Coverdale, and others.
📅Published: 1560

📜The Geneva Bible (GNV), a pivotal English translation, was first published in 1560 by Protestant exiles in Geneva. Fleeing the Catholic Mary I's reign, these scholars, under the spiritual guidance of John Calvin and later Theodore Beza, sought to continue the reformation of church and scripture. William Whittingham, along with Myles Coverdale and others, meticulously translated the Bible, which became known for its comprehensive annotations and vigorous language. This version, preceded the King James Bible (KJV) by 51 years, it was the primary Bible for 16th-century English Protestantism and influenced figures like Shakespeare and Cromwell. It was notably brought to America on the Mayflower and even inspired the design for the Great Seal of the United States. Despite its popularity, it was not printed in England until 1576, due to the political-religious climate. The GNV's legacy persisted, impacting the translation of the KJV and remaining a beloved text for its clarity, that made it easily understood by non-scholars or clergy.

📜The German Luther Bible - 1545 - Deutsche Lutherbibel (GLB) is a landmark in religious and linguistic history. The Luther Bible is a German translation of the Bible by Martin Luther, a central figure in the Protestant Reformation. Luther began translating the New Testament in 1522 and completed the entire Bible in 1534. The 1545 edition was the final version published during his lifetime. This translation was revolutionary, and it made the scriptures accessible to ordinary Germans and helped standardize the modern High German language. The 1545 edition was printed by Hans Lufft in Wittenberg, and it included Luther's final revisions and prefaces, including his famous "Warning to the Printers," which reflected his early concerns about unauthorized reprints and the ethics of publishing. While Martin Luther was the principal translator, he worked with a team of scholars including Philipp Melanchthon, an expert in Greek who assisted with the New Testament. Caspar Creuziger contributed to Old Testament translation, Justus Jonas, Johannes Bugenhagen, and others helped refine and edit the text. The German Luther Bible - 1545 - Deutsche Lutherbibel (GLB) is in the public domain due to its age.

Turkish Bible Version - Türkçe İncil Versiyonu (TVB)

📅Published: 1843

📜The Turkish Bible Version - Türkçe İncil Versiyonu (TVB) Bible is based on 19th-century Protestant translations, particularly those produced by the British and Foreign Bible Society and American missionaries working in the Ottoman Empire. One of the most influential figures was Dr. William Goodell, an American missionary who completed a full Turkish Bible translation in 1843. His work built upon earlier efforts, including the pioneering manuscript by Ali Bey (Wojciech Bobowski), a Polish-born convert to Islam and linguist serving the Ottoman court in the 17th century. These translations were crafted in Ottoman Turkish using the Arabic script, and later adapted to modern Turkish with the Latin alphabet following Atatürk's language reforms in the 20th century. The Turkish Bible Version - Türkçe İncil Versiyonu (TVB) Bible was first published in 1843 and printed by Goodell.

Vietnamese Cadman Version - 1934 - Phiên bản Cadman Việt Nam (VCV)

✍️Authors: William Cadman, John Drange Olsen, and the Christian and Missionary Alliance (CMA).
📅Published: 1934

📜The Vietnamese Cadman Version - 1934 - Phiên bản Cadman Việt Nam (VCV) Bible was the first complete Protestant translation of the Bible into Vietnamese. Spearheaded by missionaries from the Christian and Missionary Alliance (CMA), the translation aimed to provide Vietnamese believers with a faithful and accessible version of Scripture in their native language. The translation work began in the early 1920s and was completed in 1934, and it became the standard Protestant Bible in Vietnam for decades. It was printed in Hanoi and widely distributed among churches and Christian communities. The authors and translators included William Cadman (1883-1948), a British missionary and printer who was the lead translator and publisher, Grace Hazenberg Cadman, an American missionary nurse and co-translator, John Drange Olsen, a CMA missionary and translation contributor. This team diligently worked together to ensure linguistic accuracy and theological integrity, often under challenging conditions. The New Testament was first published in 1923, the Old Testament was first published in 1925, and the complete Bible (Cadman Version) was first published in 1934.

Young's Literal Translation (YLT)

✍️Authors: Robert Young
📅Published: 1862

📜The Young's Literal Translation (YLT) of the Bible is a unique work that stands out for its commitment to textual fidelity. Published in 1862 by Robert Young, the YLT was born out of a desire to render the scriptures as closely to the original texts as possible. Young, a self-taught scholar proficient in ancient languages, utilized the Textus Receptus and the Masoretic Text as the foundation for his translation. His approach was to maintain the tenses and idioms of the original languages, which often resulted in a translation that, while accurate, could be challenging to read due to its strict adherence to the source texts. The YLT underwent revisions, with a notable one in 1887, and a posthumous revision in 1898, ten years after Young's death. These revisions aimed to refine the translation without deviating from Young's original principles of literal translation. In 2020, a major revision called the Literal Standard Version was released, updating the YLT for contemporary readers while maintaining its literal approach. The YLT's legacy is its contribution to a more nuanced understanding of biblical texts, offering insights typically reserved for those familiar with the original languages.
