2. When Did the Jews Begin Using the Name?
⚖️--- Putting the evidence together the archaeological window points to 1300–1100 BCE when Yahweh worship begins to appear among southern nomadic groups (Shasu). The Shasu were nomadic, Semitic-speaking shepherds in the Southern Levant (modern Syria-Palestine) during the Late Bronze Age. From ancient Egyptian texts they were known as "wanderers" or "Bedouins," often described as brigands and associated with the deity Yahweh or YWHW, potentially linking them to early Israelites or Edomites. They lived in clans, moved with herds for grazing, and were noted in Egyptian records from the 18th Dynasty onward. They interacted with Egyptians and possibly served as mercenaries. Israel likely adopted the name during its early history when Jewish ethnic groups began to form. During the Moses era which is traditionally 1400-1200 BCE, the name Yahweh or YWHW is revealed at the burning bush in some Bible versions other than the King James Bible, and becomes central to certain Israelite sects. Not all Israelis use the name Yahweh for God; in fact, according to some Bible versions most Israelis including observant Jews, but not all Jews consider the name too sacred to pronounce, and they substitute "Adonai" (Lord) in prayers and "HaShem" (The Name) in speech. The name "Yahweh" is primarily used in some Christian contexts and by some scholars, but not by religious Jews who believe the pronunciation of God's name is lost.
